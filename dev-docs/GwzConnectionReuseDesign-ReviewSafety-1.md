## 1. Findings

### [P3-5] The suspend-aware clock rule is stated for the pool alone, but the pool's `now`, the admission-job deadline conversion and the setup helper's `Control` share one `Instant` origin under A3's single-origin invariant, which the design neither moves as a whole nor amends

**Location.** §7 "Clocks" (line 153): "The host feeds the pool from a clock that includes suspended time where the platform has one." §13's O5 amendment (235): request deadlines act in the binding; only "the instance's pool clocks (idle expiry, the deferred-connect wait and the maximum age, §7)" act in the instance. C3 (294): "the suspend-aware clock and the maximum age". Code: `ssh_worker.rs:333`, `376–380`, `1267–1268`; `ssh_admission.rs:52–54`; `agent_job.rs:66`; `https_pool.rs:47, 106`; `pool/clock.rs:45–55`. Authority: A3:12–14.

**Violated invariant.** A3:12–14 (accepted, not amended by §13): "The worker and connector share one Instant origin, so converting the pool's absolute logical deadline does not grant a fresh budget." One `now` per pool host drives request expiry, connect clocks and idle expiry alike (`pool/clock.rs`).

**State sequence.** Implement §7 as written; two readings exist and each is defective as the text stands. (a) Only the pool's `now` moves to a suspend-inclusive source. The machine sleeps for S seconds while a request with absolute deadline D is in setup. On wake the pool's `now` has advanced by S more than `Instant`; the pool sees D passed and fails the request. Its setup helper's `Control` deadline was converted as `origin + D` in `Instant` terms (`ssh_admission.rs:52–54`), so the helper still sees D as S seconds in the future: its own deadline checks are late by S, and a job started just after wake against a pool deadline computed before sleep gets a budget S larger than the pool's view — the fresh budget A3 forbids. The pool's expiry does cancel the job (`fail_request` → dispose → `Job::cancel`), so the helper is bounded, but by cancellation rather than by the deadline A3 makes authoritative, and the two owners disagree by S for the rest of the instance's life. (b) The whole origin moves (the worker's `now`, the admission conversion, `Control`, the HTTPS epoch). This is consistent and A3 holds in the new clock, but then every request-scoped clock — stall 9 s, aggregate 30 s, allocation 30 s, interaction 120 s — also counts sleep, and every request in flight across a suspend expires on wake. That is the right outcome (the peer has dropped it), but the design's O5 amendment says request deadlines are the binding's and names only three instance clocks as suspend-counting, so an implementer reading §7 literally lands on (a).

**Impact.** Bounded to the suspend event; no credential or data effect. Under (a), a stale helper budget of up to S seconds beyond the pool's deadline and a permanent S-second skew between the pool's and the helper's deadline views on that instance; under (b), an unstated change to request-deadline semantics that the timeout plan's two-clock accounting does not mention.

**Required correction (text; non-architectural).** In §7: the instance's one clock origin moves to the suspend-inclusive source as a whole — the worker's `now`, the admission deadline conversion, `Control` deadlines and the HTTPS pool's epoch — so that A3's single-origin rule holds in the new clock; state that a request in flight across a suspend therefore expires on wake by its own deadlines. In §13: amend A3:12–14's "one Instant origin" to "one origin on the instance's clock (§7)". In §16.2: note that the timeout plan's interaction accounting is unchanged, since suspended time is not interaction. Adjust the O5 amendment to say the binding's request clocks and the instance's pool clocks read one origin.

**Closure test.** With the injected clock, a jump larger than the aggregate during a setup fails that setup with `Timeout` on wake, and the helper's `Control` reports expiry at the same instant as the pool (no late budget); §15 item 11's existing jump case still expires every idle entry.

## 2. Invariant analysis

Attacks on the changed ranges; each held except as P3-5 records.

- **§8's fault split and blast radius.** Loop-alive faults (`ssh_worker.rs:879`, `:907`) can drain because the loop keeps stepping `host` while `stop` is withheld from `active`; thread-ending faults cannot, and the text now says so with the exact mechanism (`active` dropped on unwind) and A3's rule quoted. The HTTPS side is symmetric: a thread exit ends the tokio runtime and its `serving` tasks. "Its leases are discarded, and it is disposed under the cleanup owner" matches `manage`'s post-catch path (`pool.shutdown()`, then retention). Bindings still attached to a stopped instance get typed failures on new opens and detach at their operation's end; recovery builds a fresh instance under the cleanup-owner budget, refused with the named code when spent. Nothing lets a poisoned worker drive foreign exchanges.
- **§4's lease under proof.** The connection is `Leased` with `owner = Some(B)` during the proof, so T1's per-owner counts charge B, B's own cancel releases it to idle through the host, and a failed proof closes it through the release path with `Revoked` (T4). The backstop `cancel_session(B)` closes a lease under proof rather than returning it to idle (`lifecycle.rs:101`): coarser than the table's "returned to idle" but safe (one handshake). The shared job cannot run unbounded: its absolute deadline is the latest waiter's, but the stall clock is progress-based and still bounds an unanswered agent at 9 s.
- **T3's last-lessee field against every ownership-reading path.** `cancel_matching` (`lifecycle.rs:88–107`) reads `entry.owner`, which `release` sets to `None` (`:126`) — idle entries survive an abandoned binding's backstop. Eviction (`allocation.rs:95–130`) reads state and key. Per-owner accounting (T1) counts opening and leased entries by `Owner.session`; idle entries contribute nothing. The idle-reuse lease (`allocation.rs:16–45`) sets the new owner, which is the point at which "differs" is computed against the recorded last lessee by session, not serial. `closed`, `idle_closed`, `fail_request`, `start_closing` read no owner. The gwz-transport unit tests in item 6 pin all four claims.
- **The clock and the maximum age.** The idle-expiry counterexample is closed (P3-2). The age bound is applied at release on the same clock; an idle entry cannot exceed 60 s, so release-time checking suffices. The origin-sharing defect is P3-5.
- **The bound's wait and message.** A 5 s wait, LRU disposal of an unbound instance and construction all fit the admission deadline because construction needs only a spare permit (48 of 64), not the disposal's completion. The message discloses only the bound's value. See §3 for the lock the wait must release.
- **Host context `shutdown` and drop.** `shutdown` returns the report the CLI and the server need; drop disposes without one; the fork rule still forgets rather than drops (§7 172), so a child never disposes inherited sockets or joins absent threads. A gwz-py drop at interpreter exit with bindings still attached fails their exchanges typed, which is the contract's process-exit exposure (CS §16) and not new.
- **The stdio rows' trust statement.** One host context per stdio process serving one session, so cross-session reuse cannot arise there; cross-operation reuse within that session runs §4's per-binding checks; the SSH remote form uses the remote account's environment and never the client's configuration — matching the amendment's §3.4 reuse and environment clauses. The local client form is one command per process, so it reuses within the command, as in-process.
- **Eviction re-selection.** Re-selection on the no-op report and on idle/disposed transitions terminates at the waiter's deadline; it cannot close a leased entry because `retire_idle` acts on idle entries only; LRU choice across hosts under `C_total` is today's within-pool policy.
- **Round-1 invariants unchanged.** Configuration equality and its no-secret rule, per-request `gh`, SSHSIG domain separation and replay resistance, possession as the authority primitive, N2's re-read, known_hosts re-check, HTTPS credential non-inheritance, cancellation isolation, observations, retained state: none of the changed hunks touches them, and each was re-read in place in revision 1.

## 3. Risks and next action

**Residual risks below the finding bar.**
- **The bound's wait and the registry lock.** §7 puts selection, attach and the dispose decision under one registry lock and, in the next paragraph, has an operation wait for an instance "to lose its last binding". Detach takes that lock; the wait must release it, or item 11's "if an instance loses its last binding inside the deadline, it proceeds" cannot pass. One clause in C3 ("the wait releases the registry lock") removes the reading.
- **Binding session IDs must never repeat within a host context.** T3 skips the proof when the last lessee's `Owner.session` equals the new lessee's. PLD:89 calls session IDs "validated identifiers, not authority tokens"; here equality is an authority shortcut, so the IDs must be unique for the host context's life (a counter or random, never derived from a reusable core value). The driver mux generates them in-process, so no client can choose one.
- **One detacher.** §2 has the instance pump "detach finished bindings" while C4 has finish detach the binding; both must mean one path (finish marks; the pump detaches) so a binding is never detached twice.
- **Backstop cancel closes a lease under proof** rather than returning it to idle (§2 above): safe, costs one handshake, and only on abandonment.
- **Carried from round 1, unchanged:** max-rule fairness and allocation-timeout rates through a server (now named for S5.4 in §17); decision 10's alternative retaining key text; same-user exhaustion of key slots, setup jobs and helper slots (fail closed); the 256 provisional ceiling against macOS's default open-file soft limit (S5.4).
- **Two-round cap.** No finding in this round is architectural; the RemPlan's §4 stop condition is not triggered.

**Next action.** Accept revision 1 on this axis. Fold P3-5's clock-origin sentences and the A3:12–14 amendment into §7, §13 and §16.2 as a status-time correction, or carry P3-5 to TR1.4b's C3 step; neither needs another Safety round. TR1.3 can start on this design.
