## 1. Findings

### [P2-1] Pre-accept request registration has no explicit close or route-loss owner

**Location and root cause:** [Design line 44](GwzOperationSessionV1Design.md) ends endpoint construction by publishing the endpoint, *then* registers the request-local transport scope, *then* returns `Accepted`. [Line 50](GwzOperationSessionV1Design.md) makes the **constructor** transferable until its losing-result cleanup, while [lines 48 and 60](GwzOperationSessionV1Design.md) describe the **accepted operation** and its request cleanup ticket as transfer owners. The paired [capacity amendment lines 104–110](../gwz-core/dev-docs/GwzRemoteTransportCapacityAmendment.md) transfers the pre-accept epoch reservation to the operation at successful endpoint publication. Neither document explicitly defines a charged, joinable admission/registration owner between publication and acceptance, nor requires a failed registration to seal and drain its partially installed request before releasing its charge. The endpoint is published, so the blocked-constructor fix no longer protects this interval; the operation has not yet been accepted, so its normal terminal and cleanup path need not exist.

**Credible sequence:** A first network submit passes admission and constructs its endpoint. Its constructor publishes that endpoint, and request registration starts. Current core registration can insert a `ClientRequest`, install capacity, begin a mux request and await `ready()` before it returns a `TransportRequest` ([mod.rs lines 204–235](../gwz-core/src/transport_host/mod.rs); [session.rs lines 496–539](../gwz-core/src/transport_host/session.rs)). Block at that await, before `Accepted`; then close the Client or lose its route. The design transfers completed constructor/endpoint and accepted operation owners, but names no owner that joins this in-flight registration or its partially registered request. A registration error also has no specified sealed-and-drained cleanup path. The current guard's `Drop` only cancels and seals, while `finish()` is the awaited cleanup path ([request.rs lines 328–370](../gwz-core/src/transport_host/request.rs)); a timeout snapshot is not completion proof.

**Consequence:** Final close can be asserted while the registration task or request-local work remains live, or a partially registered request can lose its cleanup/epoch charge. Route loss can strand the same work without an accepted ID to inspect. The exact outcome depends on implementation, which is why the ownership transition must be specified before this interface is approved.

**Correction:** Make the whole pre-accept admission transition a charged, joinable session task from the first reservation through constructor publication, request registration and `Accepted`. Install a request cleanup ticket before any registration side effect. Close/route loss must atomically seal acceptance, join or transfer this task and its ticket, and retain the endpoint/epoch/scope charges until registration either hands ownership to an accepted operation or is cancelled, sealed and locally drained. Specify one release on every setup/registration failure, including a failed or lost `Accepted` delivery.

**Closure test:** Pause after local/mux registration and before `ready()`/`Accepted`; race explicit close, route loss, registration failure and cancellation. Verify no final close, capacity release, late acceptance or unowned registration before the task and request cleanup truly drain. Then verify exactly one cleanup and charge release, with peer operations unaffected. Repeat at the instant the successful admission hands its reservation to the accepted operation.

## 2. Invariant analysis, including failed attacks

- **Blocked constructor:** The prior Safety P2-1 attack fails under the new text. Reservation immediately creates a charged task; close seals publication, joins or transfers it, and a losing endpoint is shut down once before finality. The core epoch is pinned during this interval.
- **False cleanup completion and indefinite handler:** A five-second `CleanupReport` or a zero count before producer sealing cannot prove completion. A handler that cannot stop holds its worker/scope charges; cancellation remains operation-local, and peers can continue. This truthfully trades availability for safety without inventing a termination deadline.
- **Owner isolation and post-close reads:** Both IDs and the original route are checked on every read/control action. Same-ID foreign Clients cannot gain a handle, and the originating reachable Client retains bounded result/cleanup reads after close. Route loss deliberately provides no reattachment.
- **Effects and peer certainty:** `none` requires proof of no effect; possible sends or mutations are `may_have_applied`; peer teardown does not establish rollback. The immutable terminal may be conservatively less certain than later cleanup proof. These rules prevent a cancelled or locally drained action from being advertised as safely retryable without evidence.
- **Retention and refusal:** Fixed acceptance reservations, bounded serializer/event budgets, terminal and cleanup-marker TTLs, plus active/orphan caps prevent result release or expiry from silently retiring live physical work. Typed refusal exposes aggregate counts and caller-owned blockers but no foreign IDs. Quiescence includes constructors, accepted scopes and unfinished cleanup. The P2-1 gap is the missing owner in the handoff between those states.
- **Process loss:** The contract explicitly limits ownership to the live process and forbids inferring success or rollback after a crash. No durable recovery claim is implied.

## 3. Risks and next action

Close P2-1 in the consolidated design and core capacity handoff, then rerun the pre-accept race proof with a paused registration, not just a paused constructor. The proposed Taut shapes, wire/iroh carrier, implementation, platform evidence and release remain separate gates. This report does not treat earlier rejected drafts as accepted authority.
